# Selling kits, speakers and used gear

The marketplace is where builders sell to each other. You can list three kinds of things:

- **Kits**: everything (or most of what) someone needs to build a speaker
- **Completed speakers**: finished builds
- **Used gear**: drivers, crossovers, amps, test gear and other things you no longer need

New drivers aren't sold in the marketplace. SDLabs sells those itself from each driver's page.

You'll need Stripe connected before a listing can go live. If you haven't done that yet, start with [Getting paid: setting up Stripe](/blog/tutorials/getting-paid-with-stripe). You can still write the listing first and save it as a draft.

## 1. Create a listing

In your dashboard, open **Marketplace → My Listings** and click **New listing**.

[SCREENSHOT: My Listings with the New listing button]

## 2. What are you selling?

- **Type**: kit, completed speaker or used gear.
- **Title**: be specific. "Pair of SB17NRX woofers, 2 years old" sells better than "Woofers".
- **Built from a design**: for kits and completed speakers, pick the design on SDLabs it's based on. Buyers can then click through to the full design, measurements and reviews. If it's your own design, this is how you sell kits of it (see [Publishing a speaker design](/blog/tutorials/publishing-a-design)).
- **Driver from the catalogue**: for used drivers, pick the driver from the database so buyers see its specs and measurements. If it isn't there, you can add it ([Adding a driver to the database](/blog/tutorials/adding-a-driver)).
- **Condition**: from new down to "for parts". Used gear can't be listed as new.
- **Photos**: up to 8. The first one is the cover; drag them to reorder. Take a photo of any damage. It saves arguments later.
- **Description**: what's included, what condition it's in, how it's been used, anything you measured.

[SCREENSHOT: the "What are you selling?" section filled in for a used driver]

## 3. Price and stock

- **Price**: the price for one lot.
- **Units per lot**: how many come in one lot. Drivers are often sold in pairs, so put `2` here and set the price for the pair.
- **Quantity in stock**: how many lots you have. It counts down as things sell, and the listing shows as sold out at zero.

## 4. Shipping

- **Ships from**: your country.
- **Handling time**: how many days it takes you to get it in the post.
- **Shipping within your country**: what you charge to ship one lot domestically.
- **International shipping**: what you charge abroad. **Leave it empty if you only ship within your country.** Buyers abroad will see they can't order.
- **Each additional item**: what you add for each extra lot in the same order. If a second pair fits in the same box, this can be small or zero.

Speakers are heavy and carriers aren't cheap, so check real prices before you fill this in.

## 5. Returns

Switch on **I accept returns** if you do, and say how they work: how many days, who pays return postage, what condition it has to come back in.

## 6. Publish

Switch **Published** on and save. If something is missing, the listing is saved as a draft and you're told what to fix. The usual reasons are:

- The price is zero
- The quantity is zero
- Stripe isn't connected yet

You can pause a listing at any time by switching **Published** off.

## Your shop page

Your listings also appear on your own shop page, at the address you chose in **Selling & Payouts**. It's a handy link to share on forums.

## When something sells

You get an e-mail and a notification. Then:

1. Open **Marketplace → Sales**. The order shows as needing shipping, with the buyer's address.
2. Pack it well and post it within your handling time.
3. Click **Mark as shipped** and enter the carrier and tracking number (and a tracking link if you have one). The buyer is notified straight away.

If you need to ask the buyer something, use **More → Contact buyer**.

[SCREENSHOT: the Sales page with the Mark as shipped dialog open]

Your payout is sent 14 days after you mark the order shipped, minus the 10% platform fee (which isn't taken from shipping). [Getting paid: setting up Stripe](/blog/tutorials/getting-paid-with-stripe) explains the timing.

## If the buyer reports a problem

Buyers can report a problem if the item didn't arrive, arrived damaged or isn't as described. You'll be notified, and the payout for that order waits until it's sorted.

Open the report from **Marketplace → Problem Reports**. You can reply to the buyer and offer:

- A full refund
- A partial refund
- A refund once they've sent the item back

If the buyer accepts, the refund happens automatically. If you can't agree after 3 days, either of you can ask SDLabs to step in and decide.

Most problems are a parcel stuck somewhere or a misunderstanding. A quick, friendly reply goes a long way.
